Clicked a suspicious text-message link? Respond to what you actually did

By KYD Editorial · First published 2026-08-23 · Last editorial review 2026-10-05

English edition adapted on 2026-10-05. The original source-check date is retained below; this is not a fresh product or match investigation.

After a suspicious delivery, penalty or payment text, the next action depends on what happened after the click. Viewing a page, submitting a code, installing an app and making a payment require different responses.

Money moving now?

Contact the relevant bank, card issuer or mobile provider through its verified official channel promptly. In South Korea, the original guide also directs financial-loss reports to police at 112. The numbers in this article are South Korean contacts, not worldwide reporting numbers. Outside South Korea, use the appropriate local official channels.

Stop further interaction and preserve evidence

Do not call the number in the suspicious text, install its “cancellation” app or provide another verification code. Record the message, sender, URL, screen, installed app name and transaction notice where safe. Redact identity details, account numbers and codes before sharing evidence. Do not erase everything before recording the sequence.

If you only opened the page

Check whether anything was downloaded, installed or submitted. On Android, inspect recently installed apps and unfamiliar APK downloads without executing them. Revoke an unwanted site-notification permission if you granted it. Keep the operating system and browser updated and use Google Play Protect. Monitor unexpected logins, unsolicited codes and billing records. A click alone is not a reason to immediately factory-reset a phone, but it is not proof that nothing else happened either.

If you entered account information

Open the official app or type a verified address from a trusted device. Change the affected password and any reused versions on other services. Review signed-in devices, recent activity, recovery email and phone, and two-step verification. If you supplied a verification code, also inspect connected apps, payment methods and email-forwarding settings; do not assume changing the password addresses every form of access.

If an app or powerful permission was involved

Stop using the app and turn off mobile data and Wi-Fi on the suspected device. Protect important accounts from another trusted device. Review Android’s recent apps, accessibility, device-admin, notification-access and display-over-other-apps permissions with official guidance. If removal fails, permissions return or behavior continues, seek manufacturer or carrier assistance. Consider an official reset only with appropriate support and carefully preserved data; restoring every previous app can restore the unwanted app too.

If money or payment details were involved

Explain whether the event was a bank transfer, card payment or carrier-billed purchase; these have different support channels. Use the card’s printed contact or the official app, not a search advertisement or number from the suspect message. Ask about the provider’s available blocking and dispute procedures. Deleting a text does not reverse a transaction. Keep reference numbers and the time of each report.

South Korean reporting information

The source edition checked KISA’s 118 consultation service, police financial-loss reporting at 112, and the integrated telecommunications-financial-fraud contact 1566-1188. The linked Korean public-agency pages explain their scopes. These contacts were last checked on August 23, 2026; verify the official procedure before relying on them. They are not substitutes for another country’s reporting arrangements.

Prevent secondary harm

If contacts may have been exposed, warn close contacts about unexpected links or money requests using your name. Describe the message without forwarding the suspicious link. Ask the relevant carrier or financial institution about applicable protection and credential-reissue procedures. Do not give remote-control access or codes to someone promising recovery.
