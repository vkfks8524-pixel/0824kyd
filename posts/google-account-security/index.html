Google Account security: recovery details, devices and connected apps

By KYD Editorial · First published 2026-07-12 · Last editorial review 2026-10-05

English edition adapted on 2026-10-05. The original source-check date is retained below; this is not a fresh product or match investigation.

A Google Account can connect email, Drive, Photos, calendars and phone backups. Protecting it involves recovery access and active sessions as well as a unique password.

Start with the account’s security screen

Open the official Google Account controls directly and review recent security activity and signed-in devices. An unfamiliar device, a message you did not send or an unexpected recovery change deserves investigation. Device labels and locations are clues, not an automatic diagnosis by themselves.

Old recovery phone or emailUpdate to information you can accessLeaving another person’s contact as a temporary solution

Keep recovery information usable

Check that the recovery email and phone number are yours and still accessible. An old number or an abandoned mailbox may complicate recovery when you need it most. When helping someone else, let the account owner enter their own information and credentials; do not make yourself the default holder of their recovery access.

Add two-step verification and a fallback

Review the methods supported by the account, such as prompts, authenticator tools or security keys. Enable a suitable method and keep backup codes in a protected location that remains available if the phone is lost. A second factor reduces risk but does not make every login request legitimate.

Review connected apps

Inspect third-party services granted account access and remove those you no longer use. Review app passwords where applicable. An old integration may continue to have permissions even if you stopped visiting its website. Consider the required function and the actual permission scope.

Use a unique account password

If the same password appears on several sites, one breach can threaten other accounts. A password manager can make separate long passwords manageable. Protect its own access and recovery path too.

If you suspect account takeover

Review suspicious sessions, recovery information and connected apps as well as changing the password from a trusted device. A password-only response can leave overlooked permissions or recovery changes. Follow Google’s account-security guidance for the specific situation. Do not share passwords, verification codes or screenshots containing recovery information while asking for help.

A routine that respects privacy

Review these settings after changing devices or contact details and when account alerts appear. For family or student support, explain the screen and let the owner operate it. Helping with security does not require reading their messages or keeping their password.
