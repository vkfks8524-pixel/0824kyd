Google Drive sharing: check who can open, comment or edit

By KYD Editorial · First published 2026-07-12 · Last editorial review 2026-10-05

English edition adapted on 2026-10-05. The original source-check date is retained below; this is not a fresh product or match investigation.

Before sending a Drive link, decide who should access the file and what they should be able to do. A link’s convenience can conceal a broader sharing scope than you intended.

Restricted and anyone with the link are different

Restricted sharing grants access to authorized people rather than everyone who receives a link. Anyone-with-the-link access can extend beyond the original message because the link can be forwarded. For personal information, internal records or sensitive school and work documents, start with the narrowest appropriate scope.

Match the role to the task

A viewer reads the file; a commenter can leave feedback; an editor can change content. Distribution alone often needs only viewing. Give editing access only to people who actually need to collaborate. Available permissions and organizational restrictions depend on the file and account.

Send to one personRestricted plus that specific accountPersonal and work email addresses are not interchangeable
Publish a non-sensitive noticeConsider link access with viewer permissionsAssume the link may be forwarded
Collaborate on editingEditor access only for required participantsReview capabilities such as moving, deleting or resharing

Inspect the file before sharing

Look for names, phone numbers, email addresses, student records, addresses or evaluations. Decide whether each recipient needs that information. Then inspect general access and the people list. Do not infer permissions from the message in which the link will be sent.

Watch folder inheritance

Folder permissions can affect files inside it. If one document needs a narrower audience, review inherited access and consider a separate restricted location. Do not assume changing one visible setting overrides every inherited or organizational rule.

Review access after sending

Check that the intended recipient can open the file and that an unintended account cannot. A private-window test can help inspect general link access, but it does not simulate every organizational role. When distributing a copy, inspect the original and copy separately: their sharing states may differ.

Close temporary access

At the end of a project, event or term, remove unnecessary external accounts, reduce excess editor roles and disable public link access where no longer needed. A periodic review of important folders helps uncover old links. Follow organizational retention and external-sharing rules before moving, copying or deleting records.

Changing permissions is not recalling copies

Test the access experience after a change and update links in related documents or boards if necessary. Restricting the original does not undo information that someone already downloaded or copied. Choose the audience carefully before the first share, not just at cleanup time.
