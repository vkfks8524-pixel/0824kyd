Priority items

    Unchecked items matter more than the score

    This score does not inspect an account or detect compromise. Simple weights organize a planning checklist. A score of 100 cannot determine whether a password was leaked or recovery email compromised.

    A low score likewise does not prove hacking. Start with accounts such as email that reset other services’ passwords. Unique passwords and two-step verification have the largest weights, followed by recovery access and device review.

    Priority 1: unique passwords and passkeys

    Password reuse can turn one breach into login attempts elsewhere. Use long, different passwords and a trusted manager when needed. Supported passkeys can reduce the opportunity to enter a password on a phishing page.

    Priority 2: two-step verification and recovery

    Two-step verification adds a check beyond a password. Compare supported authenticator apps, device prompts, keys and passkeys. Prepare backup authentication and recovery codes for a lost phone; do not keep all recovery access with the password.

    Recovery details can be the key to regaining access. Replace obsolete work email or unused numbers with details you control. If an unrequested recovery change is reported, inspect activity through the official security page.

    Priority 3: sessions and security activity

    Existing sessions can remain after a password change. Inspect times, locations and device types and remove unfamiliar sessions. Review forwarding rules, connected-app permissions and payment settings as well.

    Information this tool does not request

    No account name, email, password, recovery number or verification code is needed. Avoid handing such details to a self-check site. Inspect real settings through the official app or address, not an unexpected message link.

    Limits and suspected-compromise response

    Unfamiliar logins, unrequested codes, recovery changes or payments require recovery and harm reporting before this checklist. Use a trusted device and the provider’s official compromised-account procedure.
