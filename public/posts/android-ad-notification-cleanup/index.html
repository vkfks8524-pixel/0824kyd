Unwanted Android ad notifications: find the sending app first

By KYD Editorial · First published 2026-07-13 · Last editorial review 2026-10-05

English edition adapted on 2026-10-05. The original source-check date is retained below; this is not a fresh product or match investigation.

Unexpected ad notifications can come from a website permission or a recently installed app. The first useful clue is the app that sent the notification—not a guess that the entire phone is infected.

Identify the sender without opening the ad

Press and hold the notification to inspect the sending app and its controls. If it is Chrome or Samsung Internet, review that browser’s site-notification permissions. If it is another app, inspect that app’s notification settings and purpose. Tapping the ad itself may take you to another promotion or permission request.

Review browser site permissions

In Chrome, look under Settings, Site settings and Notifications. Block unfamiliar sites that you do not want to hear from. Granting permission while visiting a news, video or download page can allow later notifications even when the page is no longer open. Removing that permission is different from deleting your photos or resetting the phone.

Review recently installed apps

Compare when the unwanted notifications began with recent installations. Simple flashlight, wallpaper or cleanup apps may contain advertising. Remove an unnecessary app using normal settings; for an app you need, first check whether promotional notification categories can be turned off separately. Back up required app data before removal.

Keep important notification channels

Android’s app settings let you adjust notifications, although names vary by manufacturer and version. Where possible, disable the promotional category instead of every notification. Completely silencing a bank or messaging app can hide information you actually need.

Do not add unknown security utilities

Start with the sender, site permissions and installed apps. Installing several unfamiliar security or cleanup apps can create more notifications and more permissions to review. Do not open unexpected APK files in the downloads folder. A factory reset is not an appropriate first diagnostic step for one unwanted notification.

Observe the result and protect privacy

Record whether new notifications still come from the same sender after changing permissions. A notification already displayed does not necessarily establish that the change failed. When helping a family member or student, ask them to show the unwanted alert; you do not need to read their private messages, photos or passwords.

Avoid granting permission just to continue

Be wary of pages that demand notifications to play a video, download a file or claim a coupon. Review requested permissions against an app’s actual purpose before installation. If ads increased after a new app, that timing is a starting point for investigation, not proof that every app in that category is harmful.
