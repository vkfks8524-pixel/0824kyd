256GB or 512GB iPhone? Plan capacity from your actual usage

By KYD Editorial · First published 2026-09-26 · Last editorial review 2026-10-05

English edition adapted on 2026-10-05. The original source-check date is retained below; this is not a fresh product or match investigation.

Photo counts alone cannot choose a capacity. Record what you use now, what remains after normal cleanup and what temporarily peaks during travel or filming. Separate device space from cloud space.

Record current usage before deleting anything

Apple’s guidance uses Settings → General → iPhone Storage. Record actual usage and the largest categories, not the advertised capacity. If you already delete files every week to stay within a small phone, current usage may understate the material you want to keep. Keep files in the plan until you have verified any backup and decided they will not move to the new device.

Estimate growth from comparable observations

The following is an illustrative usage log, not a measured record from a tested phone.

Hypothetical usage log: 120GB → 126GB in 30 days
RecordStartAfter 30 days
Total used120GB126GB
ContextNormal photo and app storageNote travel, filming or bulk deletion
Net increase126 − 120 = 6GB / 30 days

30-day equivalent growth = (ending usage − starting usage) ÷ elapsed days × 30

The example gives 6GB. A rise from 120GB to 129GB in 45 days also gives (129 − 120) ÷ 45 × 30 = 6GB. This is a comparison basis, not a prediction that every month will be identical. Log bulk deletion, cloud optimization, cache and system changes. For a falling trend, this calculator accepts 0 growth rather than negative inputs; review temporary needs separately.

Use four planning components

Planned capacity = current usage + (monthly net growth × months of use) + temporary storage + reserve

The reserve is your planning allowance for updates, apps and unexpected capture, not an Apple-recommended percentage. Avoid adding current system usage repeatedly if it is already in the observed total, but do not assume the new device’s system use will be identical.

A plan below advertised capacity does not prove it will fit. For another hypothetical case, 120 + 6 × 36 + 30 + 40 = 406GB; changing monthly growth to 2GB produces 262GB. Both exceed the 256GB label, and 406GB does not certify that a particular 512GB device is sufficient.

Cloud storage does not enlarge the phone

iCloud storage and device storage are different. Synchronization and optimization can affect local use, but purchasing cloud capacity does not increase the phone’s internal capacity. Apps, offline maps and originals needed on a flight or abroad still require local space. Review synchronized deletion before assuming a cloud item can be removed safely.

Compare capacity and subscription costs without treating them as identical

Hypothetical cost example: an extra-capacity device costs KRW 300,000 more, while an imagined cloud subscription costs KRW 5,000 per month. Thirty-six months totals KRW 180,000. The apparent KRW 120,000 saving is relevant only if the two choices meet your actual needs. This is not a current iCloud price quote. A larger phone may still need a cloud subscription for another purpose.

Check sensitivity before purchasing

Without reserve, 120 + (2 × 36) + 30 = 222GB, but changing the assumed growth to 4GB yields 294GB. Neither is a guaranteed purchase threshold. If no past usage log exists, label the small and large growth values as assumptions rather than measurements. Include travel peaks, cleanup habits and budget in the decision.

Before removing originals, use the photo-backup checks. Then compare Pro and Pro Max size and South Korean prices or the total purchase cost. The cleanup planner calculates a free-space target; it does not automatically recommend a device.
