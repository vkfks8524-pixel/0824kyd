Unlocked phone or carrier deal? Compare the full 24-month cost

By KYD Editorial · First published 2026-09-26 · Last editorial review 2026-10-05

English edition adapted on 2026-10-05. The original source-check date is retained below; this is not a fresh product or match investigation.

A lower device price can be outweighed by a costly service period or required extras. Compare the same period and the full device principal, then keep conditional benefits separate from confirmed discounts.

  2. Ask for five separate categories

Worked example, not an offer.

The A and B quotes below are hypothetical Korean-won inputs, not a current promotion, subsidy or lowest-price claim. They do not establish eligibility, legal discount rules or another country’s pricing.

Define “total” before comparing

Use the same number of service months for both quotes. Include the full device principal, combining upfront and remaining principal. Do not subtract a subsidy twice if it is already reflected. A 36-month installment schedule does not make the remaining principal disappear from a 24-month ownership comparison.

This model adds the full device principal to service costs during the selected period. It is not a cash-flow schedule of payments leaving your bank during those months. Add interest and fees separately and consult the actual payment terms and final contract.

B’s service cost is 90,000 × 6 + 55,000 × 18 = KRW 1,530,000; A’s is 25,000 × 24 = KRW 600,000. B saves KRW 400,000 on the device but adds KRW 930,000 in service, KRW 120,000 in extras and KRW 100,000 in other costs. Its net is therefore KRW 750,000 higher.

A’s average net is KRW 91,250 per month. That is not the actual monthly bill: an upfront device payment and delayed resale have different cash timing. Load the example and replace it with your own quotes. For another currency, compare both quotes in the same unit; do not mix currencies or use these examples as exchange-rate guidance.

Ask for five separate categories

  1. Final principal: upfront plus installment principal, after confirmed discounts. Do not add device installments again inside service.
  2. Pure service charges by period: initial months, later months and conditions for changing the plan.
  3. Extras: insurance, add-ons, SIM, shipping, interest and other fees. For varying extras, calculate their period totals first.
  4. Exit conditions: model early cancellation or plan changes separately. Do not assert future penalties are zero.
  5. Trade-in and card benefits: identify whether they were already deducted, when they are paid and what spending or retention conditions apply.

What changes the conclusion?

A KRW 10,000 monthly difference moves a 24-month comparison by KRW 240,000. Reducing B’s first six months by KRW 20,000 saves KRW 120,000. Changing equal resale assumptions on both sides changes your expenditure but not the gap.

The KRW 750,000 gap equals KRW 31,250 per month over 24 months. Holding everything else constant, B needs that much additional net benefit unique to B to break even. A “maximum benefit” is not confirmed money. Check eligibility, duration, extra spending and payment timing; do not count an already-applied device discount twice.

Equal price is not equal service

Compare data allowances, speed caps, tethering, roaming, bundle benefits and support. An inadequate plan can create additional purchases. The calculator covers two service-rate periods and fixed monthly extras, not every complicated contract, conditional card benefit or old-device installment. Run a confirmed-input comparison separately from a conditional-benefit scenario.
