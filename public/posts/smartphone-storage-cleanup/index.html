Phone storage full: calculate the gap before deleting photos

By KYD Editorial · First published 2026-07-12 · Last editorial review 2026-10-05

English edition adapted on 2026-10-05. The original source-check date is retained below; this is not a fresh product or match investigation.

First calculate the extra space you need, then start with files you can replace. The numbers below are planning examples—not measurements from a tested phone or a manufacturer’s free-space requirement.

A 128GB example with 8GB free

Read total and available storage from the device settings using the same units. Advertised capacity and currently usable storage are not interchangeable, and RAM is a separate category. If you choose a 15% free-space planning target, 128 × 0.15 = 19.2GB. With 8GB already free, the additional target is 19.2 − 8 = 11.2GB. The 15% figure is a user-selected planning assumption, not a universal manufacturer recommendation or an update-installation requirement.

Compare these inputs in the storage planner. It deletes nothing. If an update is your goal, inspect that update’s actual requirements instead of assuming a chosen percentage is sufficient.

Different cleanup buttons remove different things

ActionPossible effectCheck first
Delete downloaded mediaRemoves the device’s offline copyCan your account or subscription download it again?
Clear cacheRemoves temporary files that may returnIs this distinct from clearing app data?
Clear app data or storageCan remove local state, logins and dataApp-specific backup and recovery access
Clean messenger attachmentsCan remove files that cannot be downloaded againWhether the conversation backup includes media
Ordinary photo deletionMay affect cloud copies and other devicesThe service’s sync and trash behavior

Do not rely only on “backup enabled”

  1. Verify the account receiving the photos.
  2. Inspect recent and older photos plus important long videos; a sample does not prove every item is backed up.
  3. Where possible, open actual files through the service on another device rather than checking thumbnails alone.
  4. Inspect pending, paused or failed uploads.
  5. Read the scope of the service’s device-space function before using it.

Google Photos’ Free up space differs from ordinary deletion. Do not assume iCloud or another app behaves identically. Keep important originals in an independent copy outside the synchronized library and open that copy. See the photo-backup guide.

Reach the target in stages

In the illustrative 11.2GB gap, removing 4GB of replaceable offline video and 1.5GB of unnecessary downloads yields 5.5GB, leaving 5.7GB. Clearing 0.8GB of cache still leaves 4.9GB. Re-read available storage after each step: trash retention and new temporary files can mean the observed gain differs from the arithmetic. Do not jump to deleting all photos because a cache cleanup was small.

If the space still does not add up

Record total and free space, the three largest apps, each cleanup action and before/after values. Do not delete unknown system folders. Allow storage analysis to settle after a restart and consult manufacturer guidance. Factory reset is a separate decision requiring verified backup and account-recovery access.
