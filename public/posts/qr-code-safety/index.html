QR code safety: inspect the address before login or payment

By KYD Editorial · First published 2026-07-12 · Last editorial review 2026-10-05

English edition adapted on 2026-10-05. The original source-check date is retained below; this is not a fresh product or match investigation.

A QR code saves typing; it does not certify the link inside it. Inspect the destination and requested action before entering credentials, installing an app or making a payment.

Read the preview before continuing

A phone’s camera or QR app may show the destination before opening it. Compare the domain with the expected service or institution. A shortened address hides the final destination; a long or unfamiliar string alone does not prove fraud, but it should not be accepted without context.

Look for a replacement sticker

On menus, posters, delivery notices and payment signs, check whether another label has been placed over the original code. For payments or logins, confirm the official code with staff or an organizer through a trusted route. Rescanning the same altered code does not correct its destination.

SituationFirst actionStop when
A menu or public noticeInspect the sticker and preview addressA simple notice unexpectedly demands sensitive credentials
An unexpected QR code in a messageConfirm through a known contact or official siteThe sender or purpose remains unverified
A payment screenCheck the amount, business and recipientAny detail differs from what you intended

Verify each new request

A survey, attendance form or menu can become a phishing route if it leads to an unfamiliar login. Inspect the final browser address again. If it asks for an APK, new permissions or an unexpected app installation, pause and verify the official procedure. A recognizable logo is not sufficient evidence.

For QR payments

Before authorizing the transaction, check the recipient and amount on the actual payment confirmation screen. Cancel if either is unexpected and ask the business to confirm. Do not assume that the physical location of a printed code proves who receives the money.

For people distributing QR codes

Print the service name or readable destination beside the code and explain what users should expect. Provide a way to verify it independently. At the end of an event or course, remove outdated notices or clearly manage the destination so that old codes do not become misleading entry points.

Remember the underlying rule

Judge the domain, context and requested information, not the visual pattern of the code. A QR code can carry a valid link or an unwanted one. A preview and structural check can reveal clues, but neither guarantees that a website, file or transaction is safe.
