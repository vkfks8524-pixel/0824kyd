Cloud storage full? Calculate the space you need before deleting

By KYD Editorial · First published 2026-07-14 · Last editorial review 2026-10-05

English edition adapted on 2026-10-05. The original source-check date is retained below; this is not a fresh product or match investigation.

Freeing several gigabytes on a phone may not change a cloud-account warning. Identify the full location first, then account for the next upload and the space you want to retain. This guide concerns personal Google and iCloud accounts, not every business-account or OneDrive rule.

Device storage and cloud capacity are separate

iCloudPhotos, backups, Drive and account usageClearing an iPhone app’s cache addresses a different location

When the same item appears on the device and in the cloud, determine which copy an action removes. Ordinary deletion and a device-space feature are not equivalent. See the photo-storage comparison.

Inspect the account’s full usage

Google’s personal-account storage is shared across services, so inspect Gmail and Drive as well as Photos. A full account can affect uploads, email and photo backup. Work and school accounts may have different allocations or policies. Apple distinguishes iCloud storage from device storage and provides usage categories for photos, backups and documents. Do not delete an old backup just because its name is old; decide whether it may still be needed.

Add upcoming uploads and your chosen reserve

This is KYD’s planning formula, not a billing calculation or an automatic cleanup feature. Reserve space is your target, not a mandatory provider percentage.

Required cleanup = max(0, current usage + planned uploads + target reserve − account limit)

Hypothetical example: limit 15GB, usage 14.2GB, incoming photos and videos 2GB, desired reserve 1GB. The calculation is 14.2 + 2 + 1 − 15 = 2.2GB. Making room only for the upload requires 1.2GB; including the chosen reserve requires 2.2GB. These are not measurements from the owner’s account.

Hypothetical candidateAssumed spaceDecision
Photos that appear duplicated but lack verified originals2.5GBDo not prioritize deletion merely because they are large
Unneeded files with separately saved, opened copies1GBReview deletion scope before cleanup
Items with no remaining retention need1.2GBConfirm contents and scope

The illustrative 1GB + 1.2GB matches the 2.2GB target. Actual recovered capacity can differ by service and processing state. Record size rather than only item count: one video can account for substantial space.

Copying inside the same sync library may not be independent

Export or copy required originals to another location and open them. Another copy inside the same cloud account may also consume that account’s storage. An external device is useful but still has loss and failure risks. Emptying trash or deleting a backup can be difficult to reverse; check the service’s actual recovery rules rather than assuming a grace period always exists.

For shared or work material, review ownership and collaboration effects before deleting. If reported usage does not change immediately, do not repeat large deletions blindly.
